# Event Research and Verification Workflow

## 1. Establish the real search window

Determine the target location's time zone and the current local date and time. Translate relative requests into full dates, including the year and weekday. Preserve explicit dates exactly. For events ending after midnight, state both the advertised start date and the next-day end time.

Separate three horizons when useful:

1. Remaining actionable events tonight.
2. The exact requested dates, including already-ended events only when historical completeness matters.
3. Future workshops, classes, retreats, or recurring opportunities when the target window has few good matches.

Do not silently expand geography, dates, price, age, difficulty, or subject beyond what the user requested. Clearly label worthwhile alternatives outside those bounds.

## 2. Build the organizer landscape

Identify the local institutions that actually produce the activity:

- Independent venues, clubs, studios, instructors, and recurring organizers.
- City, county, library, park, school, recreation, and community-center calendars.
- Regional nonprofits, affinity groups, cultural organizations, and governing bodies.
- Festival organizers, official ticketing pages, and activity-specific directories.
- Nearby-city and regional providers when the user's geography includes them.

Record the organizer's current name, city, activity specialization, official website, recurring cadence, and participation requirements. Distinguish renamed venues from genuinely separate businesses. A provider directory is useful context, not proof that an event is taking place.

## 3. Search deliberately

Combine activity, place, exact date, year, and relevant vocabulary:

- `[activity] [city] "Saturday" "August 8" "2026"`
- `[activity] [city] "August 9, 2026" workshop OR class OR meetup`
- `[organizer] [month] [year] calendar OR schedule OR registration`
- `[specialty] [region] retreat OR intensive OR festival [month] [year]`
- `[activity] [nearby city] Sunday beginner free`

Check organizer pages, calendar views, event-specific pages, official registration providers, linked PDFs, and recent organizer announcements. Compare cached search snippets with the live page. If a first-party event page is unavailable, state that its current booking or availability could not be verified.

For niche requests, search the terminology users and practitioners actually use: social versus workshop, On1 versus On2, sesshin versus sound bath, no-drop versus race, pedal boat versus paddleboard, family play versus adults-only, or language exchange versus classroom instruction.

## 4. Prefer direct evidence

Source priority, strongest first:

1. Current event-specific organizer or venue page with the exact date and status.
2. Current organizer-owned registration or ticket checkout for the exact occurrence.
3. Official municipal, library, park, festival, or governing-body listing.
4. Official recurring schedule corroborated by an independently dated occurrence.
5. Official recurrence without confirmation that the requested instance is running.
6. Dated third-party aggregator, community post, or cached result without first-party confirmation.

Meetup can be organizer-owned when the group itself publishes the event. A generic discovery result or automatically generated recurrence is weaker than the actual organizer's event page.

Link directly to evidence near each material claim. Prefer the registration URL people can actually use, plus an organizer page when it explains details omitted from checkout. Identify whether prices, capacity, or schedule details come from secondary or historical sources.

## 5. Assign an honest status

- **Confirmed:** A current first-party source explicitly supports the requested occurrence and relevant details.
- **Recurring; occurrence unconfirmed:** The organizer advertises a cadence that mathematically includes the date, but does not confirm that instance or rule out exceptions.
- **Secondary-only:** A dated aggregator or other non-organizer source lists the event, but no first-party confirmation was found.
- **Open:** Current organizer or registration evidence indicates enrollment or tickets remain available.
- **Waitlist / sold out:** A current source says the event is full, regardless of older cached availability.
- **Closed registration:** The booking deadline passed, even when a page still displays unused spaces.
- **Canceled / relocated / changed:** The live organizer source supersedes stale snippets, cached details, or old addresses.
- **Ended:** The event occurred earlier than the current local time and is no longer actionable.
- **Unclear:** Sources conflict or a material detail cannot be verified.

Do not treat a live registration button as proof of availability when the same organizer labels the event sold out or waitlist-only. Record when capacity was checked; availability may change immediately.

## 6. Check common failure modes

- The weekday and numeric date belong to a different year.
- A registration close date is mistaken for the event date.
- A recurring calendar generates nonexistent future occurrences.
- Search results refer to a same-named city in another state or country.
- An organizer in another time zone displays times without converting to the local venue time zone.
- An old operator closed while a renamed operator still uses the same address.
- A venue moved, or a source lists a neighboring but incorrect building.
- The event was relocated, canceled, weather-dependent, or displaced by another booking.
- A ticket or class page remains indexed after checkout was removed.
- The activity requires club membership, a prior multiweek course, an affinity-group qualification, a minimum age, or a partner.
- “Free” excludes required membership, equipment, parking, venue purchase, booking fees, taxes, or a mandatory fundraising minimum.
- An optional donation is confused with a required ticket, or an expired promotional tier with the current price.
- A one-hour beginner lesson is represented as a full-day workshop.
- A sound bath, chanting event, spoken partner exercise, or yoga weekend is represented as a silent meditation retreat.
- A social walk, race, outdoor festival, or self-guided route is represented as an organized hike or group ride.
- A family program has a minimum age that excludes the user's child.

When primary sources conflict, explain the conflict, identify the stronger or newer evidence, and state what must be confirmed before attending.

## 7. Capture category-specific details

### Dance, classes, and workshops

Document style, On1/On2 or Cuban/rueda when relevant, workshop duration, instructor, prerequisite, partner or rotation policy, class cap, practice/social component, and whether a festival pass actually includes workshops. Distinguish standalone workshops, progressive class series, festival intensives, and short lessons attached to social dancing.

### Meditation and retreats

Document residential versus nonresidential, full retreat versus weekly sit, actual silent hours, exceptions for chanting/teaching/interviews, practice tradition, start/end, previous retreat requirements, meals and lodging, deposit, sliding scale, scholarships, cancellation policy, and application opening or deadline. Do not classify breathwork, music, or spoken dyads as noble silence.

### Sports, outdoor activities, and rentals

Document distance, pace, elevation, no-drop policy, skill level, equipment, rentals, helmets or life jackets, route, meeting point, membership, permits, launch fees, parking, smoke/weather, and refund or cancellation conditions. Separate guided group events from self-guided rentals or public facilities.

### Family, accessibility, and volunteering

Document minimum and maximum ages, whether a caregiver must participate, registration and screening deadlines, mobility or seated participation, sensory considerations, supplies, shift duration, transportation, and the difference between open slots and an expired application deadline.

### Language exchange, writing, games, and nightlife

Document language or game, proficiency, facilitation, speaking versus quiet practice, equipment/boards, table or cover charges, venue purchase expectation, 18+/21+ restrictions, partner requirements, accessibility, and whether new participants may join.

## 8. Rank and close the loop

Prioritize strong first-party confirmation, actual open availability, user-specific fit, convenient geography and timing, fair cost, beginner suitability, and unusual quality. Keep sold-out, canceled, closed, or uncertain events out of the main actionable ranking unless the user explicitly wants a comprehensive inventory.

When no qualifying event is verified, say so plainly. Offer clearly labeled recurring communities, nearby dates, or adjacent activities rather than pretending a weak substitute meets the request.

For plans, sequence compatible events, allow realistic travel time, identify required advance reservations, and call out important deadlines. Never claim a ticket was reserved, an organizer was contacted, or attendance was arranged unless that action actually occurred with authorization.
